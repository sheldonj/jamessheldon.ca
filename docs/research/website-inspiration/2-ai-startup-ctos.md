# Cohort 2: AI startup CTOs, co-founders and founding engineers

All sites were fetched live on 2026-10-02. Quotes are as the pages returned them. Note: several people from the suggested list have changed roles since founding their companies (Jason Liu is now at OpenAI, Eugene Yan at Anthropic, Kyle Corbitt at CoreWeave after an acquisition, Linus Lee at Thrive Capital). Their sites still show how a builder presents founder-era work. Eugene Yan (eugeneyan.com) was left out of the final 8 because he is now closer to a research/MTS profile. One useful detail from his site is still noted at the end.

---

## 1. Charlie Holtz: closest match
- **Role:** Founder and CEO, Conductor (Mac app for running teams of Claude Code/Codex agents in parallel; raised a $22M Series A). Previously Hacker in Residence at Replicate and quant at Point72.
- **URL:** https://charlieholtz.com
- **Why similar:** He builds an agentic-coding product end to end and is known for shipping. This is the same space as James's "5,300 commits co-authored with Claude Code" story.
- **Structure:** Two pages only: Home and Projects. Links to X, GitHub, LinkedIn ("ShlinkedIn") and email.
- **Hero:** "I'm working on Conductor, an app for managing teams of AI programmers."
- **Content:** A short bio covering Replicate, Point72, being born in London, Elixir, weightlifting, 40 hours of pilot training and Brown ultimate frisbee. The Projects page lists 13 projects, each as one bold name plus a short line with proof built in, e.g. "Make emojis with AI. #1 on Hacker News", "First Mac app for running Stable Diffusion locally", "David Attenborough narrates your life". No blog and no resume.
- **Visual:** Plain text in a single column on a light background. No images. The footer says "served from ewr" as a small nerdy detail.
- **Credibility:** The first sentence names the current thing he is building. Every project line has an outcome attached (#1 HN, "first"), so the list reads as a record of shipped work.
- **Steal:** A projects list where each entry is one line: **name, what it does, proof**. For example: "Cashew agent system — ~5,300 commits co-authored with Claude Code, gated by mutation testing."

## 2. Jason Liu (jxnl)
- **Role:** Developer Experience Engineer on the Codex team at OpenAI. Creator of Instructor. Earlier: founder of 567 Studios (AI consulting), Staff MLE at Stitch Fix. Angel investor and a16z scout.
- **URL:** https://jxnl.co
- **Why similar:** An applied AI builder who sells production-AI judgment (RAG, agents, evals). His career arc runs startup/consulting → frontier lab, which is the kind of move James is aiming for.
- **Structure:** Home, Writing (with categories AI / Consulting / Personal / Personal Growth), Investments, User Manual, Books, Resume.
- **Hero:** "Jason Liu here. These days, I'm a Developer Experience Engineer on the Codex team at OpenAI."
- **Content:** A "Start Here" list of curated essays (e.g. "Systematically Improving RAG", "Context Engineering Series"). A "What This Site Is" paragraph. Investments. An archive of "Old Work & Training" (consulting and courses, now closed). An HTML resume page headed "Staff ML Engineer | AI Consultant | Open Source Creator" with metrics on every line: 6M+ monthly downloads, 11k+ stars, 350M+ daily requests, team of 6–7, 2B+ users. It also includes the line "Looking for my next 4-5 year project."
- **Visual:** Material for MkDocs, so it looks like documentation: left nav, table of contents, an "edit on GitHub" link. Emoji section headers ("💥 What's up?").
- **Credibility:** Named clients (Zapier, HubSpot, W&B, Modal, Timescale, Pydantic). The claim that Instructor inspired OpenAI's structured outputs. Students from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google.
- **Steal:** A **User Manual** page ("how to work with me"), plus an **HTML resume** with a number on every bullet and a one-line statement of what he is looking for.

## Patterns across this cohort
1. **The hero is one plain sentence naming what you are building now.** "I'm working on Conductor…", "These days, I'm a … on the Codex team", "co-founder and co-creator of Amp." No slogans. James's version could be: "I'm James. I was founding engineer & CTO at Cashew Research, where I built an AI research platform and the agent system that wrote it alongside me."
2. **Minimal, text-first design is the norm.** The sites use Hugo, MkDocs or Quarto, a single column, at most a headshot, and dark mode. A polished but quiet site is enough. The content and proof carry it.
3. **Proof is numbers attached to named things.** Downloads, stars, team sizes, #1 HN, students, exits. James has strong numbers to use: ~60 packages, ~5,300 Claude-co-authored commits, SOC 2, 20+ years.
4. **A curated entry point beats a raw archive.** "Start Here" (Jason, Eugene), "Top posts" (Erik), and one flagship guide (Hamel). James should have one signature essay on his agent-assisted engineering workflow, with mutation testing as the bar for review.
5. **Low-friction contact and reusable bio assets.** These sites show a visible email, a short/long bio, a "user manual", a resume as HTML (Jason) and talks/podcasts pages. They make it easy for a hiring manager or investor to say yes and to forward the page.
