## 1. Will Larson
- **Role:** CTO at Imprint (previously Carta, Calm, Stripe, Uber; co-founded Monocle Studios).
- **URL:** https://lethain.com (blog: "Irrational Exuberance", running since 2007)
- **Why useful for James:** This is the standard example of an exec who still writes like an engineer. It's the closest model for the CTO track, and he also wrote *Staff Engineer*, so it covers the IC track too.
- **Structure / nav:** Popular | Tags | Newsletter | RSS | About. Separate pages: /about, /ways-i-help.
- **Hero:** "Hey folks, I'm Will Larson! I wrote *An Elegant Puzzle*, *Staff Engineer*, *The Engineering Executive's Primer*, and *Crafting Engineering Strategy*." The About intro is understated: "I'm a developer and I write a little bit."
- **Content types:** Leadership essays, engineering strategy, systems thinking, career advice, technical tutorials, book notes. The archive is chronological.
- **Leadership credibility:** Four books. He publishes strategy documents as artifacts (*Crafting Engineering Strategy*). There's a long trail of writing, plus talks.
- **Visual design:** Serif type, dark text on a light background, a plain dated list of posts, almost no branding.
- **Open-to-work / help signal:** The "Ways I'm available to help" page is a precise list of yes and no. Yes: "Answer concise questions via email…", "Double opt-in introductions…", and he *might* do blurbs and podcasts. No: first-job advice, debugging, guest posts, investing. "Email … is the only reliable way to reach him."
- **Steal this:** A **"Ways I can help" page** with explicit yes/no lists, e.g. "Hiring a CTO/founding engineer: yes. Fractional advising on AI agent systems / SOC 2: yes. Recruiter spam for junior roles: no." It sets expectations and signals seniority.

The migration to Substack is also a warning: own your domain and archive.

## IC vs leader positioning: how they handle it
- **Say both in one line.** Kaplan-Moss: "software developer… and engineering leader." Larson's About calls himself "a developer" despite being a CTO.
- **Give the switching a name.** Charity's pendulum essay makes moving between tracks a sign of strength. Tanya's *Staff Engineer's Path* shows IC leadership as its own senior track.
- **Let the writing show which track.** Goedecke (IC) writes about shipping and agents, Larson (exec) about strategy and orgs, Eugene Yan about both. Readers infer the level from the topics.

## Open-to-work / hire-me / advising signals seen
- **Explicit "ways I help" pages:** Larson (/ways-i-help, with yes and no lists) and Kaplan-Moss (/help).
- **Public résumé link plus email invitation:** Goedecke.
- **Booking calls to action:** Swizec ("Email me to book" workshops and speaking), Hamel (courses and consulting proof).
- **Quiet signals:** a Speaking page (Rands, Eugene Yan), an Investing page (Gergely).
- **No literal "I'm looking for a job" banner on any of them.** The closest is a public résumé and a help page. James is actively searching, so he can be more direct than this cohort, but in the same plain, specific tone.

## Patterns across this cohort
1. **Writing is the portfolio.** All eight lead with essays, not project grids or skill bars. Leadership credibility comes from published reasoning: books, strategy docs, design-doc templates. **James's 54 ADRs are his version of this**, and a curated, anonymised set of 3–5 ADRs or strategy write-ups would make him stand out.
2. **The hero is one plain sentence of identity plus proof.** "I wrote X, Y, Z" (Larson); "co-creator of Django, and engineering leader" (Kaplan-Moss); "20 years of building production code" (Swizec). No taglines like "passionate about…".
3. **Navigation is short:** 4–7 items, typically Writing / About / Talks or Speaking / Books or Projects / Newsletter / Contact or Help. The "Start Here" page (Eugene Yan) and "Ways I help" page (Larson, Kaplan-Moss) are the two high-value extra pages.
4. **The AI angle comes through a dedicated series, not a buzzword.** Ubl's "Agentic AI" section, Goedecke's agent posts, Yan's applied-LLM patterns, Hamel's evals. A focused "How I built an agent-driven engineering workflow (5,300 Claude-co-authored commits)" series would put James in this group directly.
5. **Minimal, type-led design that the owner controls.** Serif or simple sans, light backgrounds (Swizec's dark theme is the exception), one accent colour, almost no imagery beyond a headshot. Charity's move to a stock Substack shows the cost of not owning your domain and design. A fast, typographic, self-hosted Next.js site is both the norm here and a demonstration of James's skills.
